# Curriculum–Industry Alignment in Data Science

### A Research Study on Industry Skill Requirements and University Curriculum Coverage

## 📌 Overview

This research examines the alignment between Data Science industry job requirements and skills represented in university curricula. It compares standardized skills across both datasets to identify areas of alignment, differences in skill representation, and potential curriculum coverage gaps.

The project combines data preparation, Excel-based computational analysis, comparative evaluation, and research interpretation to understand how industry skill requirements correspond with academic curriculum representation.

## 🎯 Research Objectives

- Identify frequently represented skills in Data Science job descriptions and university curricula.
- Compare skill-category emphasis and prevalence across both datasets.
- Evaluate curriculum coverage of industry-identified skills.
- Examine selected emerging technologies and differences in skill rankings.
- Identify potential areas for further curriculum review.

## 📊 Dataset Overview

| Dataset | Documents | Standardized Skills |
|---|---:|---:|
| Industry Job Descriptions | 130 | 81 |
| University Curricula | 80 | 79 |

The datasets were processed and standardized to support comparative analysis across 14 skill categories.

## 🛠️ Methodology

1. **Data Collection:** Compiled Data Science job descriptions and university curriculum documents.
2. **Skill Standardization:** Consolidated skill terminology into standardized labels and categories.
3. **Skill Matching:** Compared industry skills with their curriculum counterparts using exact, normalized, and conceptual matching.
4. **Computational Analysis:** Used Excel to calculate skill frequencies, prevalence, alignment, coverage, and ranking differences.
5. **Statistical Analysis:** Conducted an exploratory chi-square analysis and calculated Cramér's V.
6. **Interpretation:** Examined areas of alignment and divergence while accounting for methodological limitations.

## 📈 Analytical Framework — Eight Analyses

1. **Skill Frequency Analysis** — Identifies frequently represented skills.
2. **Category Frequency Analysis** — Compares the relative emphasis of skill categories.
3. **Prevalence Alignment Analysis** — Measures alignment across comparable skills.
4. **Skill Gap Analysis** — Examines the direction and magnitude of prevalence differences.
5. **Curriculum Coverage Analysis** — Evaluates direct and conceptual curriculum representation.
6. **Emerging Technology Alignment** — Examines curriculum representation of 14 selected technologies.
7. **Rank Difference Analysis** — Compares relative skill rankings across datasets.
8. **Chi-Square Test and Cramér's V** — Explores the relationship between skill category and curriculum coverage status.

## 🔎 Key Results

- **33.18% Overall Alignment:** Average prevalence alignment across 47 comparable skills—not the percentage of the curriculum that aligns with industry.
- **30.86% Strict Coverage:** 25 of 81 industry skills have direct curriculum representation.
- **58.02% Broad Coverage:** 47 of 81 industry skills have direct, partial or conceptual representation under your matching framework.
- **14.29% Emerging-Technology Alignment:** 2 of the 14 selected technologies have direct curriculum coverage; this is not a measure of every emerging technology.

These results describe the selected datasets and matching framework; they should not be generalized to all Data Science roles or university curricula.

## ⚠️ Limitations

- The analysis is based on 130 industry job descriptions and 80 university curricula.
- Skill matching includes conceptual judgments, which may influence coverage results.
- The 14 emerging technologies form a focused, not exhaustive, subset of the industry skill inventory.
- The chi-square analysis is exploratory. Since 40 of 42 expected cell frequencies are below 5, the usual expected-frequency condition for the Pearson chi-square approximation is substantially violated. The p-value should not be treated as definitive evidence, and Cramér’s V should be interpreted cautiously.
- The findings reflect the selected datasets and methodology and do not establish causal relationships or represent every Data Science job or university curriculum.
